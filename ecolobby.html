README

Executive Summary

How can societies continue economic development without exceeding the planet’s ecological capacity?

This project analyzes global ecological footprint trends by combining socioeconomic indicators, resource consumption metrics, and biocapacity measurements across 182 countries.

Using Python-based data engineering workflows and interactive Tableau deployment, this analysis investigates how economic development patterns influence environmental demand and identifies the primary drivers behind global ecological overshoot.

The project integrates statistical preprocessing, missing-data management, exploratory analysis, and business intelligence visualization to transform complex sustainability datasets into accessible decision-support tools.

The Core Finding: Global ecological deficits are driven primarily by carbon-intensive infrastructure and consumption patterns, rather than simple land resource scarcity. While many biological resource categories remain near equilibrium, fossil-fuel-driven economic systems create the largest imbalance between human demand and Earth’s regenerative capacity.

Core Research Questions & Key Insights

1. How does economic development influence ecological demand?

  • Method: Exploratory analysis comparing ecological footprint measurements with socioeconomic indicators including HDI, income classification, and economic development metrics.

  • Insight: Higher levels of economic development correlate strongly with increased ecological demand. Developed economies generally demonstrate larger ecological footprints due to increased energy consumption, infrastructure intensity, and carbon emissions.

2. Which resource categories contribute most to global ecological overshoot?

  • Method: Sector-level decomposition of ecological footprint components including carbon emissions, cropland demand, grazing land, forest products, and fishing grounds.

  • Insight: Carbon footprint represents the dominant contributor to ecological pressure. Agricultural and biological resource categories frequently remain closer to sustainable thresholds, while energy-intensive infrastructure creates the largest global imbalance.

3. Can socioeconomic indicators predict sustainability outcomes?

  • Method: Multivariate statistical modeling using economic development variables and ecological footprint metrics.

  • Insight: Socioeconomic indicators explain substantial variation in ecological outcomes (\(R^2 = 0.78\)). However, individual economic predictors cannot be interpreted independently due to strong relationships between development indicators, requiring careful feature selection and validation.

Data Engineering & Pipeline Development

The Missing Data Challenge

The Global Footprint Network dataset contained incomplete socioeconomic records across countries, creating challenges for consistent global comparison.

The Problem: Removing incomplete observations would disproportionately exclude countries with limited reporting infrastructure, introducing geographic bias into downstream analysis.

The Solution: I developed a regional median imputation workflow that preserved global coverage while maintaining realistic socioeconomic distributions.

The pipeline:

  • Validated missing-value patterns across global records
  • Grouped observations by geographic region
  • Applied regional statistical baselines for missing values
  • Preserved country-level representation for visualization and modeling

Statistical Analysis & Feature Engineering

Resolving Multicollinearity in Socioeconomic Indicators

Initial modeling revealed strong relationships between economic classification variables and human development measurements.

HDI demonstrated predictive significance independently, but introducing overlapping categorical variables reduced interpretability due to shared variance.

The Analytical Fix:

  • Removed redundant predictors during regression modeling
  • Evaluated economic variables independently
  • Used categorical classifications primarily for visualization
  • Preserved interpretability for stakeholder communication

Interactive Dashboard Development

The final Tableau dashboard transformed complex ecological accounting metrics into accessible visual stories.

Dashboard capabilities include:

  • Country-level sustainability profiles
  • Biocapacity deficit visualization

By combining Python-based data engineering with Tableau visualization, this project demonstrates the complete analytics workflow:

Raw Global Data → Statistical Processing → Validated Metrics → Interactive Decision Support

Conclusion: Connecting Data, Development, and Sustainability

This analysis demonstrates that ecological sustainability challenges cannot be explained by population size alone. Instead, environmental pressure is strongly shaped by infrastructure systems, consumption patterns, and economic structures.

Through automated data cleaning, statistical validation, and interactive visualization, this project provides a reproducible framework for translating complex sustainability datasets into actionable insights.
